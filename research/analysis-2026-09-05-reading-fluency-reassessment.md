# Alif reassessed for Arabic reading fluency

September 5, 2026. Strategic reassessment and proposals; no application, production, or learning-policy changes made.

**My recommendation is to organize Alif around supported, progressively more independent reading of meaningful texts. Preserve its vocabulary and memory machinery as support for that activity.** The project has built a substantial receptive vocabulary. Its next problem is converting that knowledge into a reading experience worth returning to. Further scheduler improvements can help, but they do not by themselves supply that experience.

This is more specific than “read more.” Stian has tried bilingual short stories and *Momo*, found them motivating, and still found the reading too difficult to sustain. The missing intervention is a carefully designed bridge between sentence review and independent prose. A library button and a bilingual translation have not been enough.

The learner's clarification in this conversation is central: limited energy is partly a response to the present experience and the feeling of being in a “trough of mediocrity.” Time is genuinely scarce, and history and poetry compete for it. But willingness to spend that time on Arabic is not a fixed input. The revised approach must test whether visible comprehension, interesting content, and manageable support increase voluntary engagement.

**What I examined and what the evidence covers.** I inventoried 209 research, design, memory, and project-history Markdown files; reviewed the key learner interviews, research syntheses, longitudinal analyses, passage experiments, corrections, and implementation contracts; and scanned 410 distinct user messages across 62 available top-level Codex histories. Claude's project directory currently exposes the memory link, not its original conversation transcripts, so Claude history is represented through those memories and the project's extensive decision records. This is a broad synthesis with targeted close reading, not a claim to have read every historical tool call or independently replicated every experiment.

I also analyzed the verified September 5 local production backup and 208 daily interaction-log files. The database opens read-only and its SHA-256 is unchanged after analysis. The recorded review history runs February 8–September 4: 209 calendar days, approximately seven months. The learner's overall Arabic study may cover nine months, but these records do not establish nine months of equally detailed Alif telemetry. Exact token-presentation evidence begins July 27.

The checkout is behind the deployed September 3 maintenance experiment. I checked the newer `5f212bc` protocol and the completed September 3 conversation rather than assuming the local `CLAUDE.md` describes today's behavior. The newly deployed changes are part of the baseline, not recommendations rediscovered here. Reproducible calculations and scope inventories are in [the supporting directory](reassessment-2026-09-05/).

**There is substantial learning, not evidence of a general collapse.** The fresh analysis finds:

| Observation | Result | Interpretation |
|---|---:|---|
| Stored word-review rows | 73,539 | Word judgments, not 73,539 independent exercises or experiments |
| Canonical content lemmas with a reading-sentence judgment before September 3 | 2,949 | A substantial base built and exercised |
| Current canonical content lemmas labeled `known` | 2,629 | A scheduling state, not a proficiency certification |
| Latest available reading-sentence judgment after a gap of at least seven days was clean | 2,393 of 2,849 tested lemmas | Demonstrated delayed recognition, with the limitations below |
| Same measure after at least 30 days | 1,302 of 1,778 tested lemmas | A weaker long tail; many words have not had this test |
| August 4–September 2 recognition after 7–14-day gaps | 87.6%, n=1,225 | Higher than 77.8% in the preceding 30-day period |
| Recognition after 14–30-day gaps | 80.4%, n=794 | Higher than 73.4% in the preceding period |
| Recognition after gaps of 30+ days | 74.0%, n=561 | Similar to 72.6% in the preceding period |

These are descriptive comparisons across changing word mixes and learning policies. “Clean” means rating 3/4 without recorded confusion. The gap clock is time since the preceding recorded reading-sentence judgment for that canonical lemma; it cannot rule out intervening book, listening, quiz, or external exposure. The latest qualifying success may also precede a more recent short-gap failure. It is evidence of demonstrated recognition, not a claim that all those words are currently mastered or would be recognized unaided in any book.

The longitudinal comparisons stop before the September 3 exposure-only policy. After that change, `ReviewLog` alone no longer contains all exposures. Future gap calculations must include the exposure ledger; otherwise the apparently “cold” interval can be false. Absolute state counts also differ from older reports because this audit excludes inert vocabulary and variant rows shadowed by a canonical lemma. They must not be interpreted as a loss of words by subtracting unlike totals.

**The larger mismatch is between the practice supplied and the ability sought.** Across the available logs, approximately 98.5% of reading-review events refer to material stored as generated sentences or generated passages. The August 4–September 2 window contains 794 logged reading-review events and approximately 7,746 mapped running tokens—about 258 a calendar day. Repeated material is included. Token totals use current mappings and are an approximate volume measure, not a reconstruction of every historical display. Missing or edited mappings and incomplete older event semantics limit precision.

The new book reader has six recorded page/passage completion events, all associated with page 1 of its *Momo* chapter, most during early testing; the last is August 22. These do not establish six distinct pages read. Nor do they establish absence of external reading. Stian's answer in this conversation supplies that missing context: currently almost none, with intermittent earlier attempts.

The important finding is therefore not a speculative learning-rate deficit. It is that the app has supplied a great deal of contextual word practice and very little sustained, self-reinforcing prose reading. A reader needs to connect clauses, track referents, recover a meaning after ambiguity, retain the thread of a paragraph, and increasingly do all of this without reconstructing an English sentence. Knowing many individual words helps, but does not make these operations automatic.

**The plateau may partly be designed into the experience.** An adaptive review system deliberately keeps selecting uncertain or overdue material. As ability improves, it supplies another uncertain item. That can be good memory practice while making the subjective experience of learning remain remarkably unchanged. Review debt is also renewable: finishing today's work does not finish a story or reveal that a formerly inaccessible page has become readable.

This is a plausible mechanism, supported by the learner's repeated descriptions, not a demonstrated causal diagnosis. Earlier conversations already asked for meaningful, beautiful, surprising material and for the pleasure of returning to a real text and understanding it. They also documented fatigue even on textbook pages containing familiar vocabulary. Those observations suggest both a motivation problem and a processing/automaticity problem; neither is solved merely by adding more new lemmas.

The most useful feedback loop to build is: finish a meaningful piece with appropriate help; encounter its important language again; return with less help; continue into something new. Each part should have value to the reader. It should not require completing an indefinite review queue first.

**The research supports changing the balance, with important qualifications.** A 2025 extensive-reading meta-analysis found a modest positive average effect, including comprehension and fluency outcomes. But 80 of its 82 interventions concerned English, and delayed follow-up was sparse. It supports a reading component, not a guaranteed Arabic-specific effect size or a prescribed daily percentage. [Sangers et al., 2025](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10648-025-10068-6).

Pure incidental exposure is not a reliable replacement for deliberate learning of every difficult word. A separate meta-analysis found that only a minority of targeted unknown words were learned through meaning-focused input, with considerable variation between conditions. This favors a combination of reading and selective vocabulary practice. [Webb, Uchihara & Yanagisawa, 2023](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/language-teaching/article/how-effective-is-second-language-incidental-vocabulary-learning-a-metaanalysis/E38E3468FD2090B1FA3051051DE8E70C).

The broad evidence for spacing remains a reason to keep distributed review. It does not establish one ideal retention target, number of daily introductions, or number of exposures for this person. [Kim & Webb, 2022](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/lang.12479).

The local research is more decisive about morphology than about adding still more context variety. In July's longitudinal analysis, new-sentence/seen-form recognition was 88.1%, versus 80.9% for new sentences with new forms. The adjusted new-form association was approximately −4.8 percentage points; verbs also showed substantial difficulty. These are observational effects. The [subsequent context audit](context-diversity-decision-2026-07-31.md) established that Alif already supplies abundant context diversity; a generic “more varied sentences” intervention is largely redundant. More encounters with a root do not establish that every related surface or meaning is readable.

**1. Change the main outcome to independent reading, while retaining vocabulary as a supporting measure.** Define progress as being able to understand more demanding or longer previously unread Arabic passages, at an acceptable pace and with less help, in the genres that matter to Stian. Record comprehension, pace, assistance, and willingness to continue separately. Avoid a single composite score that hides their tradeoffs.

Two complementary comparisons make progress both visible and credible. Periodically revisit a meaningful passage that used to be difficult: this demonstrates a personal achievement and may help automaticity. Separately read a fresh, comparable passage that has not been specifically rehearsed: this checks transfer. The old passage is not an independent proficiency test, and the new passage should not always come from the exact material optimized by the scheduler.

The measurement can remain light. Before translation, form a mental account of what happened or what the author is saying; afterward, judge whether the account was right. Occasionally use one or two prepared meaning checks to catch missed relationships, with no written Arabic or production exercises. An honest learner does not need constant quizzing. Honesty still cannot make an unobserved misunderstanding observable, so small diagnostic checks are useful when the learner agrees to them.

Measure first-pass Arabic reading separately from checking translations, browsing roots, background time, and rereading. Existing `response_ms` combines too many activities to serve as a fluency score. There is no basis here for assigning a CEFR level or promising a date for fluent literary reading.

**2. Build a bridge that succeeds on the difficult days.** Start with one coherent scene or historical episode rather than a whole novel as the unit of commitment. Choose material for intellectual interest, continuity, and actual reading success. A difficult *Momo* page does not become easy merely because the interface reveals its English.

For an initial bridge, a passage might contain 50–120 Arabic words in total, presented in short coherent portions. That range is a starting design choice, not a scientific threshold. The amount should shrink if the learner cannot follow it even with support. Preserve surrounding context and give a brief recap on returning. Do not split a sentence in a way that conceals who a pronoun refers to or what a clause modifies.

Offer three useful levels of support on the same material: original Arabic with unobtrusive help; closely aligned Arabic/English; and, where necessary, a clearly labeled simpler Arabic retelling before returning to the original. Translation first is a legitimate preparation option. An adapted retelling is a bridge, not evidence that the original has been read independently, and must not silently replace or distort the source.

The reader should be able to finish with a small amount of genuinely flowing Arabic. After understanding a short portion, hide the English and reread it once for meaning without checking every token again. Then either stop at a satisfying boundary or continue. Purposeful rereading here serves fluency and enjoyment; it does not justify restoring repetitive understood cards throughout SRS. Research on assisted repeated reading makes this a reasonable trial, but does not prove transfer from a rehearsed passage to new Arabic prose. [Taguchi, Takayasu-Maass & Gorsuch, 2004](https://nflrc.hawaii.edu/rfl/item/89).

Supply both achievable reading and occasional aspirational reading. Easy material develops flow; a short, well-supported poem or classical excerpt reconnects the work with the ultimate goal. Poetry should not be the principal measure of general prose fluency. As the learner wants to read history anyway, accessible Arabic historical narratives, biographies, or contextual explanations of poetry are especially promising candidates. Select one main strand for continuity and a small optional strand for delight, rather than asking the learner to manage a curriculum across many books.

**3. Let the next meaningful reading determine part of the curriculum.** The existing frequency core is valuable background infrastructure. It should supply broad gaps and break ties, while the next few passages identify immediate obstacles. Preteach only the highest-value blockers, then return to the text promptly. Do not load a chapter's entire unknown vocabulary into acquisition.

The operational distinction should be:

| Encountered difficulty | Response |
|---|---|
| Recurring, important word or expression needed in the next passages | Brief introduction if needed; later contextual retrieval within the existing intake budget |
| Familiar lemma in an unfamiliar inflection or attached form | Explain the displayed form; practice that form and a small useful contrast |
| Known individual words but unclear sentence relationship | Explain the clause, referent, construction, or idiomatic meaning |
| Rare detail that is easy to understand from a gloss | Supply the gloss and continue; no automatic learning obligation |
| Interesting literary or cultural allusion | Optional short explanation that serves the reading |

Rank potential practice by likely benefit to intended reading, recurrence across nearby texts, current fragility, and learning cost. High predicted failure risk alone is not high learning value. Some repeatedly failing low-relevance words should receive less immediate attention than an expression that unlocks the next scene. Preserve long-term classical interests when judging relevance; general modern frequency alone is not an adequate filter.

This builds on existing primary-story curriculum membership and guided-reader semantics. It does not require replacing the lemma database, discarding old knowledge, or creating an independent SRS card for every possible form. Seeing a word remains useful exposure even when that exposure does not impose another scheduled obligation.

**4. Teach the bottleneck revealed by the text, especially forms and sentence structure.** Root awareness and morphological enrichment already exist. The next useful step is selective application: here is the exact word you saw, here is its familiar base, and here is the part responsible for the changed meaning or role. For broken plurals, weak verbs, participles, and attached pronouns, this may be much more useful than another unrelated sentence containing some form of the lemma.

Vary difficulty deliberately. Initially hold the troublesome form constant in another clear context; later change the form or make the context less predictive. After a genuine lexical mix-up, teach the distinguishing feature and later check a new context. A context that makes the wrong candidate impossible can aid recovery, but success there alone does not establish general disambiguation skill.

Add brief explanations of structures encountered in actual reading: clause boundaries, pronoun reference, negation and exception, relative clauses, participial constructions, and verbal expressions whose meaning depends on a preposition. This is receptive grammar instruction embedded in reading. The existing grammar tables and exposure scores are a useful starting point, but aggregate sentence success does not independently establish mastery of the construction. Research syntheses identify grammatical as well as lexical knowledge as important correlates of reading comprehension; correlations do not tell us which intervention will help Stian most. [Jeon & Yamashita, updated meta-analysis, 2022](https://benjamins.com/catalog/bpa.13.03jeo).

Function words can remain exempt from ordinary vocabulary scheduling while their contribution to meaning is explicitly taught. A missed negation or pronoun relationship may matter more to comprehension than an unknown descriptive noun. Likewise, a known dictionary gloss is not proof of recognizing a new contextual sense or a multiword expression. Add lightweight phrase/construction evidence when needed, rather than forcing every problem into “forgotten lemma.”

**5. Keep tashkīl and translation as adjustable support, with an explicit path toward independence.** The target includes ordinary unvowelled prose, so eventually recognition must work there. But taking away help globally can lower the amount of useful reading and amplify ambiguity. A study of 54 English-speaking Arabic learners found benefits associated with vowelized instructional materials; an Arabic L2 dissertation also found benefits from partial vowelization and context for comprehension. These do not establish an optimal fading schedule. [Midhwah & Alhawary, 2020](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/modl.12642); [Aljohani, 2022](https://yorkspace.library.yorku.ca/items/2cd22bf9-31ac-4cde-9c69-b190108a08f2).

Use help where it permits fluent access, then check selected portions without it. High canonical FSRS stability is insufficient reason to assume a newly encountered form can be read without vowels. The local observation that hidden-vowel words score better is confounded because the system hides vowels for stronger words. Do not interpret that association as evidence that removing vowels improves learning.

Translation should be available in the arrangement Stian has repeatedly requested: close alignment, little scrolling, preserved punctuation, persistent position, and minimal interface chrome. Optional audio synchronized with an already understood passage may help phrasing if it feels useful. It should not require a separate listening curriculum or another large content-generation project.

**6. Make the reader's behavior compatible with reading for meaning.** Several pieces already exist: the bilingual reader, guided inline glosses, exact-range completion, canonical vocabulary, token-level form evidence, and offline caching. The priority is a successful end-to-end reading experience using these pieces.

The first screen should make continuing the current text as easy as starting reviews. Resume the exact place and assistance settings. Prepare the next small set of passages before the session. Verify the entire offline continuation path, not just that the first page loads. Root detail and morphology should be available without covering the whole passage or forcing repeated Arabic/English scrolling.

I would also reconsider one intentional reader policy: a lookup is currently closely tied to declaring a miss. In supported reading, a tap may mean “show the root,” “confirm this sense,” or “explain the construction.” Preserve explicit misses and their learning value, but distinguish inspection from failed recognition. Similarly, an unmarked token read beside visible English is not automatically an independent retrieval success. This is a proposed semantic change for review, not a claim that the existing behavior was accidental. The current guided mode already prevents many unintroduced glossed words from entering acquisition, and the clean reader's Box-2 floor already differs from a full successful FSRS review.

Reading should remain possible while a vocabulary mapping or enrichment task is pending, provided the text and displayed explanation are trustworthy. Scheduling credit should wait for verified identity. Preserve the strong mapping protections; avoid making complete enrichment of every word a prerequisite for enjoying a carefully prepared passage. For an initial pilot, use a small fully verified set so this architectural separation is not a prerequisite for starting.

**7. Keep the September 3 maintenance improvements, but broaden what success means.** That live package caps automatic cards at four actionable due words, narrows new intake, prioritizes fragile knowledge, supplies bounded confusion-context rescue, and makes qualifying trivial mature collateral exposure-only. It also holds automatic passage reviews off following their earlier suspension. Those are meaningful workload changes; two days of use cannot establish their retention effect.

The new clarification changes the strategic interpretation. Thirty cards a day is a recent coping pattern, not an eternal goal. Calmer maintenance is useful, but optimizing only for its sustainability could institutionalize the plateau. The important longer-term question is whether maintenance leaves room for enjoyable reading, and whether reading itself increases willingness to return.

Retain the current policy as the maintenance component while testing a bounded substitution of reading for some review time. Do not require reading on top of an unchanged daily quota. Do not immediately halve maintenance or claim that glossed pages replace it one-for-one. Start with roughly 20–30% of the learner's current Arabic session time allocated to supported reading; increase the share only when comprehension, voluntary engagement, and retained important vocabulary support that decision. This is an experimental starting allocation, not an optimum derived from the dataset.

The present 95% FSRS target is not a promise that every word can be maintained at 95% real-world recognition within any available time budget. Longer term, explicitly budget maintenance by relevance and demonstrated fragility, accepting that retaining every encountered word maximally may cost more than it contributes to the reading goal. Quantify this tradeoff before changing retention parameters globally. The existing late-delivery and exposure semantics make an immediate universal FSRS retune difficult to interpret.

**The next experiment should test the reading bridge itself.** I would prioritize one small complete delivery over another broad research program: prepare enough carefully chosen material for 7–10 brief sessions, integrate continuation and appropriate help, connect a few recurring blockers to existing practice, and show evidence that the material becomes easier. Momo can be one candidate, but choose an easier passage or a scaffolded version if it still defeats the intended experience.

The September 3 package should first receive its planned operational check. Then record the start of the reading substitution as a new phase. There is no pedagogical reason to wait 60 days before reading; there is a methodological reason not to attribute later results solely to an unchanged maintenance experiment. A protocol amendment should be explicit before implementation.

| Stage | Practical question | Decision |
|---|---|---|
| First 3–5 reading sessions | Can a short meaningful portion be understood with help, followed by a smoother Arabic reread? Does the interface disappear enough to read? | If not, fix support, material difficulty, or continuity before adding vocabulary or session length |
| Two weeks | Does the learner want to return? Are completed new portions accumulating? Is help or checking time falling on comparable material? | Keep or adapt the bridge; allow optional additional reading, while preserving the initial time-budget comparison |
| Four weeks | Is there improvement on fresh matched passages and acceptable maintenance of important old vocabulary? | Increase reading share if the balance is favorable; repair the specific loss if it is not |
| Six–eight weeks | Can the learner sustain longer coherent reading with less support, and does it now compete successfully for scarce reading time? | Make this the main experience if it succeeds; investigate the remaining decoding, lexical, or syntactic bottleneck if it does not |

Use two primary outcomes: performance on fresh comparable passages, and voluntary return to the reading. Keep vocabulary retention as a guardrail. Include several short benchmark passages so a single unusually easy or hard text does not drive the verdict. Compare similar genre, length, support conditions, and difficulty. Report comprehension alongside pace: faster reading that loses the meaning is not improvement.

On the maintenance side, preserve the frozen older-word cohort and report test coverage as well as accuracy. A risk-based selector changes the mix of words tested. A drop in the selected sample's accuracy may reflect harder sampling; a rise can reflect omitted difficult words. A small distributed sample of old words within ordinary sentence review can guard against this, without withholding useful exposure merely to manufacture long gaps.

Warning signs for the new reading phase are repeated inability to finish even a short supported portion; predominantly reading English while reporting Arabic independence; growing lookup obligations that refill acquisition; improvement only on rehearsed passages; and a sustained decline in matched older-word recognition accompanied by shrinking maintenance coverage. The response should be local—easier original material, better explanation, a smaller intake, or more targeted maintenance—not automatically demanding more effort. Existing operational safety rules for the maintenance experiment remain in force until deliberately revised.

This is a staged N-of-1 product experiment. It can give useful evidence for this person, but a simple before/after comparison will not isolate every component or establish that reading caused all improvement. Motivation, material, time availability, and accumulated knowledge change together. Record actual exposure and help; avoid elaborate causal claims unsupported by the design.

**What to stop treating as settled.** Several older summaries turn useful heuristics into universal rules: a fixed vocabulary threshold for reading, a fixed ideal success rate, a fixed number of encounters, or root familiarity translated into precise knowledge credit. Schmitt and colleagues found a broadly continuous relationship between lexical coverage and comprehension rather than a sharp threshold; a later replication could not fully reproduce the original 98% result. High coverage still helps, especially for unaided reading, but these figures are not permission gates or guarantees. [Schmitt, Jiang & Grabe, 2011](https://www.lextutor.ca/cover/papers/schmitt_etal_2011.pdf); [Kremmel et al., 2023](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/lang.12622).

The project's [July readiness audit](analysis-2026-07-28-learning-update.md) already identified inflated OCR-derived coverage and advised supported reading. The next step should implement a workable transition, not repeat that advice with a new coverage percentage. English word-family thresholds, Arabic lemma counts, root families, and actual surface recognition must remain distinct.

Passage research also needs a more careful interpretation. May's “four times slower” result was later revised after idle time and event attribution were examined. June's favorable result used FSRS stability changes as part of its retention argument; those are outputs of the model, not independent delayed learning outcomes. August's [more careful word-level comparison](analysis-2026-08-09-recovery-and-story-efficiency.md) involved only 11 new story cards and five later target reviews. It supported provisional layout/length improvements, not a general verdict on the best route to literary fluency. Disconnected or target-packed micro-stories are not equivalent to engaging sustained reading. Repetition can also be useful for fluent comprehension even when it yields fewer distinct scheduled lemmas per minute.

The rich interaction history remains valuable. But 91.4% of the 10,285 exact token-evidence rows inherit a sentence-level judgment rather than an explicit token mark. Only 70 rows carry a nonempty failure-cause label in this snapshot. Neither fact invalidates honest feedback; both limit conclusions about specific lexical, form, or grammar failures. Many tokens in one sentence share context and one overall judgment. They are not independent participants, and missing optional cause tags are not evidence that a cause never occurred.

A leaner research practice would keep one current strategy document and a ledger separating hypothesis, implemented policy, replay/simulation result, observed learning result, and superseded interpretation. Use prediction models to find actionable problems; do not replace the scheduler merely because another model has a better retrospective AUC. Defer broad frequency-list rebuilds, global context-diversity tuning, large morphology dashboards, unrelated language expansion, and additional listening infrastructure unless they remove a concrete obstacle in the selected reading path.

**If I were starting from scratch today**, I would make the central object a short, interesting text with persistent context and adjustable assistance. The system would choose a manageable next portion, explain the actual obstacles, arrange a little distributed practice, and make return and continuation effortless. I would retain Alif's verified Arabic identities, history, and scheduling beneath that experience. The first success criterion would be completing a real piece with increasing independence and wanting another one. That is the most direct way to test the unlock Stian believes will lead to sustained growth, while acknowledging that the unlock may emerge gradually rather than at one vocabulary count.
