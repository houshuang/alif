## Interpretation

The new sequence supports the learner's report that some familiar phrases resolve without needing meaning options: six answers were correct and reported clear before those options. However, readiness is subjective and final choices can still include additional Arabic reading or elimination. There is no normative threshold here establishing automaticity.

Vowel-condition medians were nearly equal for the three correct items in each condition. Different phrases, word histories, and error exclusions remain confounded. These phrases are generally more familiar and contextualized than the isolated volunteer item. The result fits, rather than contradicts, the learner's account that some unvowelized forms are fine and other words require trying several readings. Do not remove vowel support globally or treat the earlier experience as disproven.

The separate choice timer demonstrates why combined timing was difficult to interpret: رسائل أختي هناك took 6.886 seconds before choices, then 11.562 seconds to choose, despite a report that the meaning was already clear. Other choice intervals were approximately 1–3 seconds. This extra time cannot simply be assigned to Arabic word access; comparison of similar English alternatives or unmarked distraction are possible explanations, not established causes.

The story's initial pace is descriptively close to the first assessment's 28–30 whitespace tokens per minute, with successful broad comprehension again. These are three different short vowelized passages, not a calibrated longitudinal benchmark. The later flowing reread and enjoyment are promising evidence that the experience can change within a brief encounter.

**No explanation use was recorded.** The learner spent about a minute on the support screen identifying two chunks, where Arabic remained visible. Before that screen, English comprehension choices supplied parts of the story's meaning, including the flood explanation. Thus the later reread cannot be described as either an effect of the supplied explanations or a pure unaided-repetition effect. Familiarity, continued reading, self-resolution, and semantic cues from questions may all contribute. A clarifying question about what helped has been sent. There was no new comprehension check after rereading, so do not claim independently verified preserved/improved comprehension on the second read. There was also no fresh transfer passage.

## Practical next step

Use the next few sessions for the reading bridge: a small meaningful portion, quick help for words that genuinely block the meaning, one optional purposeful reread, then a small new continuation. Keep tashkeel available and avoid requiring full word-by-word mastery before the next part. Distinguish “unknown meaning” from “familiar word that took time to resolve” when choosing later contextual practice.

The success criterion is not speed alone: clearer first encounters with new continuation, manageable effort, and desire to keep reading matter. A smoother familiar passage is an intermediate success worth using, but if all benefit remains confined to rereading, adapt support or difficulty rather than claiming general fluency growth. No new broad battery is needed to justify trying this bounded reading experiment. Use the here/there clarification above for proportionate contextual contrast practice; woman/man is a confirmed selection slip.
