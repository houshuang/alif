Longitudinal learning analysis · opportunity 5

Alif already varies context. The unresolved question is whether it varies too much at once.

A frozen replay of every word-level reading event shows that context diversity is already a dominant property of acquisition and mature review. The clean experiment originally proposed is severely underpowered because same-form sentence sets are scarce. The actionable uncertainty is the interaction between a new context and a new Arabic surface form.

Cutoff 30 July 2026, 13:58 UTC · database SHA f98ad15b…2784a · all words count, whether primary or collateral

Decision

Do not add another global diversity multiplier and do not force a familiar-sentence control arm now. Preserve the current selector. Use the exact-form pilot and protocol-v2 token evidence to separate context novelty from surface-form novelty; only then consider a narrow same-form preference for second-day confirmation.

Material implication. The frozen sentence pool contained only ten completed acquisition episodes with an anchor plus two semantically distinct, same-form alternatives suitable for a clean trial. Generating form-controlled sentence sets—not adding another selector multiplier—is the prerequisite for testing this interaction.

4. A clean retention experiment cannot answer quickly

The more feasible micro-randomized design found 747 non-overlapping matched opportunities, about 9.58 per active day. Its next-review endpoint is still too sparse to detect plausible small effects promptly.

5. What the literature does—and does not—validate

Butowska-Buczyńska et al. varied cue sentences while holding foreign forms constant. Across six experiments, variable cues improved learning when learners retrieved meanings, including after 24 hours, and spacing magnified the effect. Learners nevertheless believed constant cues were better. This strongly supports Alif’s retrieval-before-reveal flow and its high context turnover.

Frances et al. held encounter count constant and spread pseudowords across 1, 2, 4, or 8 texts; diversity improved recall, recognition, and meaning matching. Conversely, Norman et al. found no word-form benefit: low diversity improved performance in familiar contexts, while high diversity improved transfer to unseen contexts. A later anchoring study again found null form effects and a familiar-context advantage.

These experiments predict directions, not Alif-sized effect amounts. They use controlled pseudowords or Finnish forms, repeated intentional translation retrieval, and short post-tests. Alif uses naturally inflected Arabic, whole-sentence self-assessment, incidental collateral exposure, and FSRS intervals. A numeric claim that Alif is learning “faster than the literature” would therefore be false precision.

Next evidence gate

  1. Use protocol-v2 rows to separate new-context from new-surface failures.
  2. Let the seven-day exact-form pilot establish whether deliberate same-form retrieval improves later transfer.
  3. Measure the second-day acquisition confirmation specifically.
  4. If changing both context and surface is harmful, add a non-blocking preference for a different sentence with the same surface form when available.
